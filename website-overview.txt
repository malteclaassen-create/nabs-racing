NABS RACING LEAGUE - FEATURES
=============================

A rundown of what the site does. Everything on the public side comes out of a
database, nothing is typed in by hand on the pages, so when a race gets imported
every table updates on its own.


THE PUBLIC PAGES

Home
  The first thing you see. It shows the last race result (the podium and which
  track it was), who is leading the championship, who won the last race, and
  when the next race is with a live countdown. Under that you get the top of the
  driver table, both constructor tables (Tier 1 and Tier 2), and the points
  progression graphs.

Drivers
  The full driver standings, everyone sorted by total points. There is a filter
  to switch between all drivers, only Tier 1, only Tier 2 or only reserves, and
  a tick box to hide people who have not scored yet. Click a driver to open his
  profile.

Driver profile
  A stats page for one driver - wins, podiums, best and average finish, poles,
  places gained, a season form chart, a head to head against another driver,
  and a race by race table. Both races of a sprint weekend count: a sprint win
  is a win, a sprint podium is a podium and a sprint driven is a start, here,
  in the Hall of Fame and in the Cockpit - as do the fastest laps of both
  races. The grid numbers and the poles stay per round (a sprint grid is not a
  qualifying result). In a season
  with sprint weekends the sprint races show up too: the season form draws the
  sprint as a dashed line behind the race line, with the sprint result under
  each round's chip (the switch there is Race / Qualifying, no Sprint tab), a
  Sprint view on the head to head, a Sprint
  column in the race by race table (with the weekend's points split), and
  Sprint Wins / Sprint Podiums stat tiles that break the totals down.

  Track Strengths (on the same page, under the race by race table) shows which
  kind of circuit suits the driver: high-speed, heavy braking, power, flowing,
  technical or street. Every race is measured against its own field (where they
  finished among the finishers, where their best lap ranked), so 50 is the
  average driver of the grid and higher is better. There is a radar of the six
  types, a row per type with the circuits behind it, a tile per circuit, and a
  rival picker that lays another driver's shape over the radar. All seasons of
  the league or just this one. Each circuit's types come from the admin Tracks
  tab; circuits the site knows start on a default reading.

Constructors
  The Tier 1 and Tier 2 tables, both with a round by round points breakdown. The
  rounds that get dropped (see the rules file) are shown crossed out. Each tier
  also has its points graph and the team line-ups.

Transfers
  The transfer market. Every driver's team round by round as a grid (a reserve
  drive for a team is marked, a round not driven is a dash), the team changes
  of the season as a list, and the moves already booked for rounds still ahead
  marked as upcoming. Everything here comes from the results themselves and
  from the transfers the admin has entered, so it is the same answer the
  standings give.

Races
  The full calendar. Finished rounds can be clicked and show the complete result
  - finishing order, the grid spot and how many places each driver won or lost,
  the fastest lap, and for Tier 2 a separate column with the re-ranked Tier 2
  position and points. Special events (like the Le Mans night) have their own
  tab.

Live timing
  This connects straight to our Assetto Corsa server and shows live sectors,
  best and last lap, gaps, tyres, pit stops and who is out on track right now.
  If no session is running it just says it is waiting for the server.

Telemetry tips
  The lap comparison on the Tools page has a Tips panel. It is written for the
  driver of the slower of the two laps: where the gap goes corner by corner,
  then the three corners that cost the most as cards - "You brake 22 m earlier
  than Siggsta and still carry 8 km/h less through the slowest point" - with
  the numbers behind it, a small speed trace, and what the quicker lap did
  there as a suggestion. When one habit (say, early braking) is behind most of
  the lost time it says so once. Corners carry their real names (T4 Roggia)
  once they are named in the admin Tracks tab; until then they are numbered.

Sign up
  This is our replacement for Apollo. People log in with their Discord account
  and set themselves to Accepted, Declined or Tentative for each upcoming race.
  Every answer also gets posted and updated automatically in the Discord
  channel. While logged in a driver can also pick his own country here, and that
  then shows as his flag everywhere on the site. Profile edits (name, flag,
  picture, bio, number, socials, stat tiles) apply to the driver's row in every
  league they race in, not only the one their login is tied to.

Other things
  - Dark mode and light mode (button in the top right).
  - It can hold more than one season. Right now it is Season 7, but old seasons
    stay readable through a season picker once there is more than one.


THE ADMIN AREA

This sits behind a PIN (top right, "Admin"). From there I can:
  - Import a race. Either upload the result JSON from Content Manager or pull it
    straight off the server. It matches the in-game names to our drivers by
    itself and I just confirm them.
  - Edit any race afterwards. There is a live preview that shows exactly how a
    change moves the standings before I save it. This is also where I add
    steward time penalties.
    A round that has no result file (say the server never wrote one and all we
    have is the message in Discord) is typed in here too: add the drivers in
    finishing order, mark the DNFs, save.
  - Transfers tab: the same round-by-round grid the public page shows, with a
    Transfer button on every driver. A team change is entered against a ROUND:
    pick one still ahead and nothing changes today, the move applies itself
    when that round's result is saved; pick one already driven and the rounds
    since are re-attributed and their constructor points move, which the
    dialog spells out before you confirm. Booked moves are listed with a Take
    back button, and the grid exports as CSV. A "Needs attention" card lists
    teams with more than two seats and drivers who exist twice in the season;
    Merge folds a duplicate row into the one that stays (results, answers,
    login, Steam id and empty profile fields move over, the other row goes).
  - Attendance tab, Activity: who is still turning up. Every driver against
    every round that has been saved, split into Tier 1, Tier 2 and the reserve
    pool. Two numbers per driver: the rounds they RACED (a DNF counts, a DNS
    does not) and the rounds they ANSWERED the sign-up for, whatever they said.
    The status runs on both, so a reserve who only ever answers still counts as
    present; a round with neither is Quiet, three is Inactive. It also flags
    who said yes and never started, and exports as CSV.
    Each driver also has a Progress label the staff set by hand: Full time,
    Possibly reserve, Deserving, In progress, Tentative. That is the reserve
    spreadsheet moved onto the site. Nothing computes it and no table scores
    off it. It saves as you pick it and belongs to that season only.
  - Add and edit drivers and teams, including team colours and uploading team
    logos.
  - Manage seasons.
  - Create or announce upcoming races and events (the Discord webhook they
    are posted through is set under System -> Discord).
    A race day can be one race or, for an F2 style event, a sprint plus a
    feature race - each with its own lap count. Both are named in the Discord
    announcement and on the sign-up page, and after the event both
    classifications are stored: the import asks which race the file is, and
    the results view gets a Feature/Sprint switch. The sprint scores the same
    points as the feature race, added to that round.
  - Give one race its own points table (race editor, "Custom points for this
    round"), for a double-points finale or any one-off format. The whole
    round pays by it, sprint included, and the tables mark it with a star.
  - Name a season's champion by hand (Seasons tab) when the league decided
    the title by its own rule rather than by points. The final table puts
    that driver first with a note.
  - Set a fastest lap bonus per season (Seasons tab). The driver with the
    fastest race lap gets it on top of their finish, if they finish. The
    standings mark those cells with FL.
  - Content Check (/content-check, no login): a driver who gets kicked with
    "Checksum failed" picks their assettocorsa folder, the browser hashes the
    files the race server checksums (each entry car's data.acd, the track's
    surfaces.ini) and names the one that differs. The reference hashes are read
    off the race server itself, so "correct" is what the server will compare
    against, not a guess. Nothing is uploaded - the files are read and hashed
    in the browser. Linked from the Race Info page under the downloads.

  - Set points by hand per driver (Seasons tab, "Manual points") for anything
    the results cannot show on their own - a pole award, laps led, a stewards'
    deduction - or type a season total straight off the league's own sheet.
    The driver standings mark those rows with an M; constructor points are
    not affected.
  - Import the qualifying session of a round (Quali on the results editor).
    Once it is there, that session decides who took pole everywhere on the
    site - the Hall of Fame, the profiles, the track pages. Without one the
    pole is whoever started from grid slot 1, so a reverse-grid feature race
    should always have its qualifying imported.
  - Per circuit (Tracks tab): its type (up to three of high-speed, heavy
    braking, power, flowing, technical, street), which the driver profiles'
    Track Strengths group by, and its corner names with where they sit in the
    lap (percent), which the telemetry tips use.
  - Change the admin PIN.


HOW IT IS BUILT (short version)

The front end is React (Vite and Tailwind). The back end is Node and Express
with a Prisma database. Right now it runs on SQLite, which needs no setup, and
it can move to PostgreSQL when we put it on a real server. The sign-up login
uses Discord, and there is a Discord webhook for posting the attendance. All the
points and standings are worked out on the server, so the numbers are always the
same no matter where you look.
