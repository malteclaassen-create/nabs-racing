NABS RACING LEAGUE - SCORING AND RULES
======================================

DNS, DNF and DSQ all score 0.


FASTEST LAP

A season can pay a bonus for the fastest race lap (set per season in the admin,
Seasons tab, "Fastest lap"; 0 = no bonus). The driver who sets the fastest lap
of the race gets it on top of their finishing points, but only if they finish
the race - a car that sets the lap and then retires gets nothing, and nobody
else gets it instead. It counts for the driver and for the team they drove for
(in the Tier 2 table it sits on top of the re-ranked points). On a sprint
weekend the sprint and the feature race each pay their own. On the round by
round tables a cell with the bonus in it is marked FL.


CUSTOM POINTS FOR ONE ROUND

A round can have its own points table (set per race in the admin, "Custom
points for this round", typed like the season's table: points for P1, P2, ...).
Every result of that round is scored by that table instead of the season's,
the sprint of a sprint weekend included; the fastest lap bonus comes on top as
usual. A double-points finale is just the season's table doubled. On the round
by round tables such a round is marked with a star.


CHAMPION BY RULE

If a season's title was decided by a rule the points do not express (for
example "whoever finishes ahead in the finale is champion"), the admin can name
the champion on the Seasons tab. That driver tops the final table with a note
saying why, and the Hall of Fame, the season honours and the champion seals
follow the table. Points are not changed.


POLE POSITION

Pole is the fastest driver of the qualifying session. Once a round's qualifying
result is imported (Results tab, Quali), the site takes the pole from there for
the Hall of Fame, the driver profiles, the track records and the race facts.
Only a round with no qualifying on file falls back to whoever started from grid
slot 1 - which is also how a pole is recorded by hand for the old seasons. A
reverse-grid start is never a pole.


SPRINT WEEKENDS

Some rounds run F2 style: a short sprint race and then the feature race on the
same evening. Both races pay the full points table above, and both count for
the drivers and for the constructors (the Tier 2 re-ranking is done in each
race on its own). The sprint's points are added to that round, so a sprint
weekend is one round worth up to 70 points. For the drop scores it is still one
round. On the round by round tables a sprint weekend is marked with an S.

A SPRINT WIN IS A WIN. Both races of the weekend count as races won or on the
podium: on the driver profile, in the Hall of Fame lists, in the Cockpit
achievements and in the countback that splits two drivers on equal points. What
stays per round is everything measured against a race night - starts, average
and best finish, the grid numbers and the places gained - so a sprint weekend
is still one start. The Sprint Wins and Sprint Podiums tiles on a profile are
the breakdown of the totals, not a separate count.


MANUAL POINTS

Some things a league pays points for cannot be read off a classification - a
pole award, leading a lap, most laps led, or a deduction the stewards handed
out. For those the admin can set points by hand per driver (Seasons tab,
"Manual points"):

  Bonus   is added to the points the rounds pay. Negative for a deduction. The
          rounds keep scoring normally, so the bonus keeps working as the
          season goes on. A total can never fall below zero.
  Total   is the season total typed in whole, for figures taken straight off
          the league's own sheet. It wins over the computed points and over
          the bonus.

For an archived season stored as official final totals, a hand-set number wins
over the stored sheet as well - what the admin types today is what the table
shows.

Both only touch the DRIVER standings - the constructor points stay with the
rounds their drivers scored them in. Every row that was touched is marked with
an M in the driver standings, with a footnote saying the round columns may not
add up to the total. Leaving both fields blank puts a driver back on the normal
scoring.


TIERS

We run two tiers, Tier 1 and Tier 2, plus reserves. A reserve can stand in for a
team when one of the regulars cannot make a race.


DRIVER STANDINGS

Everyone (Tier 1, Tier 2 and reserves) is in one big table, sorted by total
points. The points come straight from where the driver actually finished.


TIER 1 CONSTRUCTORS

For each team you just add up the real race points of its two Tier 1 drivers. If
a reserve drives for a Tier 1 team that race, his points count for that team.
Nothing gets re-ordered here, it is the real points.


TIER 2 CONSTRUCTORS

This one works a bit differently. For each race we keep only the drivers whose
team is a Tier 2 team and drop everyone else. Tier 1 drivers, and reserves who
are not driving for a Tier 2 team, are taken out completely - they do not hold a
spot. Then we re-order just those Tier 2 cars by where they finished and give
out points by that new order. So the Tier 2 points follow the Tier 2 finishing
order, not the overall one. A reserve driving for a Tier 2 team scores for that
team.


DROP SCORES (BEST 9 OF 12)

At the end, the three worst rounds of each driver and each team are dropped and
do not count toward the total. In a 12 race season that means your best 9
results count. A race you did not score in counts as a zero and is dropped
first. Rounds that have not happened yet also count as zero, so nothing real
gets dropped until late in the season. On the round by round tables the dropped
rounds are shown crossed out.


TIME PENALTIES

A steward penalty is added as seconds onto the driver's total race time (for
example 5 or 10 seconds), not as a number of positions. After the penalty the
car drops behind everyone it now sits behind on time. A car that was a lap down
or that retired is not affected, because it is not on the same time anyway. The
penalty gets entered in the admin and the order and the points update by
themselves.
